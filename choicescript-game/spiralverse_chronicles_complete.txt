[b]Welcome to the Spiralverse![/b]

The world shimmers into existence around you—or perhaps you shimmer into existence around it. Hard to tell, really.

A raven perches on what might be a dimensional threshold, or possibly just a very pretentious doorframe. She regards you with eyes that have seen too many timelines to be impressed by any single one.

"Welcome to my—our—well, to Avalon," Izack stammers, managing a smile. His hands flutter near several pouches of what might be spell components, or possibly emergency snacks.

Aria tilts her head at a precise 23-degree angle. "We predicted a 93.7% chance of your arrival. I'm recording variables for an experiment in inter-dimensional hospitality. Don't mind the measuring instruments."

Polly adds, "I give you three days before you accidentally summon a pudding elemental." She winks at you conspiratorially. "The current record is two days."

You follow Aria and Izack through the Hall of Cartography. Maps of impossible places line the walls—some showing dimensions that exist only on Tuesdays, others depicting the emotional geography of forgotten dreams.

A haunting melody floats through the air, notes that seem to paint colors in your peripheral vision. You follow the sound through corridors that rearrange themselves politely to accommodate your journey.

In a grove where the ceiling is open sky (despite being indoors), Zara practices weaving reality with song. Her voice rises and falls like breathing, and the plants around her dance in response—literally dance, some of them attempting what might be a waltz.

She smiles when she sees you, warmth radiating from her like sunlight. "New blood? Come, let me show you how harmonics reshape existence!"

Polly mutters from a nearby branch, "Prepare for a soundtrack. She does everything to music. *Everything.* I once watched her harmonize with a door hinge for forty minutes."

You join Zara in song, tentatively at first, then with growing confidence. The plants lean toward you, curious. One particularly enthusiastic fern attempts to applaud.

"Dimensional cascade occurs at 73.2% probability when variables misalign," she notes, eyes glittering with the joy of precision.
